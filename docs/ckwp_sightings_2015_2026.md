# California Killer Whale Project — public sighting reports, 2024–2026

**Purpose:** archive of public CKWP Facebook posts documenting Bigg's killer whale encounters
in the central California coastal region (Monterey Bay area and Southern California). These posts
are publicly visible and cited here with attribution. They are NOT part of the CKWP's private
scientific records; pod identifications and encounter details should be confirmed with CKWP before
use in any publication.

**Why this file exists:** the project needs sighting location data to calibrate MARS hydrophone
detection range (finding #44). CKWP public posts often include named individuals, dates, and
approximate locations — and unlike the MBWW monthly sightings lists, they carry no explicit
redistribution restriction as they are posted publicly.

Source: California Killer Whale Project Facebook page,
https://www.facebook.com/CaliforniaKillerWhaleProject

## 2026-04-03/04 — Northern Channel Islands (Southern California)

**Source:** CKWP Facebook post, ~April 14 2026. Screenshot `gradio_oct2015_tmp_052109.png`
(now archived in `docs/`). Photos by Isabel Gutowski, Condor Express, 4/3/2026.

**Animals (4/3):** CA122Bs (4), CA122Ds (2), CA212Bs (2). CA122D (≈19 years) traveling with
1-year-old calf CA122D2. CA212B traveling with ~9-year-old juvenile CA212B1. Successfully
hunted ≥3 California sea lions.

**Animals (4/4):** CA137 "Hercules," CA40 "Xena," CA27Es, CA140s ("Emma's pod," 4), CA138s
(~6, adult male CA138A unconfirmed), CA27, CA27C, CA27F (3), CA216Bs (4), CA216Cs (2).
At least 29 killer whales in 4 sightings. Multiple pods traveling west.

**Location:** Northern Channel Islands / Santa Cruz Island area (southern California).
**NOT** in the MARS detection area, but useful context for pod movement patterns and confirms
multiple matrilines active along the California coast in spring 2026.

**Relevance:** April 2026 is a processed campaign month (April 26 encounter, findings #17ff).
The Channel Islands are ~400 km south of MARS; this is background context, not a candidate
for acoustic correlation.

## 2015-11 — Monterey Bay killer whale sightings (MBWW)

**Source:** Monterey Bay Whale Watch, *Marine Mammal Sightings List November 2015*,
`https://www.montereybaywhalewatch.com/sightings/slst1511/` (retrieved 2026-09-01).
**⚠️ SAME COPYRIGHT RESTRICTION as September and October lists — do NOT commit the sightings
data to this PUBLIC repo without Nancy Black's written permission.**

| Local date | KW count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 11/5 a.m. | 20 + 5 | Two groups logged separately |
| 11/12 a.m. | 7 | |
| 11/13 a.m. | 7 | |
| **11/22–23** | **not logged** | **Our acoustic Episode A: 88+ orca calls overnight** |
| **11/28** | **not logged** | **Our acoustic Episode B: 6+ above-threshold windows** |

**Key pattern — now four consecutive months:** every confirmed acoustic encounter falls outside
whale-watch operating hours. Every daytime sighting day produces no acoustic detection.

| Month | Sighting days | Acoustic detections on those days | Overnight acoustic detections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sep 2015 | 4 (9/1, 9/11, 9/14, 9/16) | 1 (9/16 — 17 min after predation event) | 2 (9/27, 9/28) |
| Oct 2015 | 5 (10/6, 10/11, 10/14, 10/23, 10/25) | 0 | 2 (10/7, 10/26) |
| Nov 2015 | 3 (11/5, 11/12, 11/13) | 0 | 2 (11/22-23, 11/28) |

**False negatives on sighting days:**
- **11/5:** only 5.67 h coverage (23.6%) — partly a coverage gap, partly range
- **11/12 and 11/13:** good coverage (~18 h and ~24 h); genuine misses — range or crypsis

**The acoustic record is consistently capturing encounters the visual record cannot see.**
Overnight presence with daytime silence is consistent with Bigg's acoustic crypsis during
active hunting (they go quiet when prey has acute hearing) and/or animals moving offshore
at night to the canyon where MARS sits.

## 2016-02 — Monterey Bay killer whale sightings (MBWW)

**Source:** Monterey Bay Whale Watch, *Marine Mammal Sightings List February 2016*,
`https://www.montereybaywhalewatch.com/sightings/slst1602/` (retrieved 2026-09-04).
**⚠️ SAME COPYRIGHT RESTRICTION — do NOT commit to public repo without written permission.**

| Local date | KW count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Feb 12 | 13 | Partial coverage that day (66%) |
| Feb 20 | 9 | Full coverage — **0 acoustic** |
| Feb 21 | 6+6 | Full coverage — **0 acoustic** |
| Feb 24 | 8+8 | Full coverage — **0 acoustic** |
| Feb 25 | 6 | Full coverage — **0 acoustic** |
| Feb 28 | 6+6 | Full coverage — **0 acoustic** |

**Key finding:** Zero orca detected acoustically despite 5 fully-covered sighting days
with 6-16 animals visible. This is the strongest false-negative case in the campaign —
coverage cannot be the explanation. **Leading hypothesis: Bigg's acoustic crypsis during
gray whale calf predation.** Late February is exactly when the first gray whale calves
arrive in Monterey Bay — prey with acute hearing — and Bigg's are known to hunt silently
in this context. The hydrophone at 29 km may simply be outside detection range for silent
animals, even when whale-watch boats can see them inshore.

**Interannual note:** January 2016 had 41 confirmed orca just weeks earlier. The Jan→Feb
acoustic silence, with animals visually present throughout February, is a striking contrast
that reinforces the behavioral/crypsis explanation over a seasonal-absence explanation.

## 2024-04-02 — Minke whale predation, CKWP (public Facebook post)

**Source:** California Killer Whale Project (CKWP) public Facebook post, April 4, 2024
**URL:** https://www.facebook.com/CaliforniaKillerWhaleProject/posts/pfbid02ZmkqikUdGpD1oZWYh55HJrgebYTtjYb51M1kBXysciPrND2xTXo4xiZ8XaGiTMcwl
**Event date:** April 2, 2024

**Summary:** 6th minke whale predation ever documented in Monterey Bay. 35+ Bigg's killer
whales including named individuals **CA138** (lead reproductive female), **CA45**, **CA39**,
and CA138's calf CA138E. First observed as 4-5 females/juveniles, then 13+ approached
including 3 large males. 10-minute chase of juvenile minke whale; drowning technique used.
Observed by MBWW naturalist Rose Franklin (CKWP volunteer). Photos by Rose Franklin Photography.

**Named individuals:** CA138, CA45, CA39, CA138E — CKWP photo-ID documented.

**Context for acoustic archive:** April 2024 is within the MARS archive period. September 2024
was reviewed (0 orca detections). April 2024 has not yet been processed — flag for future
embedding and inference. A minke whale predation with 35+ orcas vocalizing is exactly the
kind of event that might produce a strong acoustic signal.

**Prey type note:** Minke whale predation — a cetacean with different acoustic sensitivity
than gray whale calves or dolphins. The diel calling pattern during this event (if detected
acoustically) would be a valuable data point for the prey-type hypothesis (finding #58).
