## 4. The number we'd most like your view on: detector recall

Of the **18 confirmed orca calls** in September, only **4 windows in the whole month** cleared
`orca_v10`'s F1-optimal operating threshold of +2.31 — and **one of those four was a dolphin**
(`MARS_20150928_063349` @355 s, v10 = 2.349, the month's second-highest score).

So at the operating point: **3 true orca calls detected out of 18 present ≈ 17% recall**, at
75% precision.

**More than half the confirmed calls were found only by a second, low-threshold pass** — the
lowest was v10 = 0.205 and is unambiguously a real call by ear.

Practical reading: the threshold is well suited to **finding encounters** (one call above
threshold is enough to know where to look) but would **badly undercount calls** if used for
call-rate statistics. That distinction matters for how we frame any seasonal analysis.

## 5. August's single candidate — the one clip we'd like you to hear

`MARS_20150828_212219` @325–330 s (**2015-08-28 21:27:44 UTC**), figure
`figures/gradio_aug28_2015_ORCA_325s_wid255405.png`.

- Duane: looks and sounds like an orca, but **lacks the higher frequencies** and is
  **completely isolated** — no other detection within three hours in either model.
- Scores: v10 1.406 (5th in the month), v4 0.512. **Below both operating thresholds.**
- A diagnostic low-threshold run confirmed the isolation is real: within its own recording
  the next-highest window is −1.155; across 20:00–23:00 (2,160 windows) the 99th percentile
  is −1.99.
- **Both readings remain open:** a distant orca (range strips high frequencies first, and
  Bigg's are acoustically cryptic), or a distant dolphin high-passed the same way.
- Caveat we're keeping honest about: it stands out locally partly because that evening was
  quiet. Month-wide it is mid-pack among windows that turned out to be dolphins.

**An August orca would be notable** — every confirmed Monterey Bay Bigg's event on record is
April–May.

## 7. Storage and throughput — what an 11-year pass actually costs

Measured, not estimated. Useful if this ever needs a resourcing conversation.

### The share

| | |
|---|---|
| thalassa `/PAM_Analysis` | **51 TB total, 47 TB used, 4.3 TiB free (92%)** |
| Duane's four directories | **~1.4 TB, about 2.7% of the volume** |
| Reclaimed by retiring old resamples | 96% → 92% used |

The 24 kHz tree (Google Multispecies Whale model) and 48 kHz tree (OrcAI model) were deleted on
2026-08-30. Both covered only a few selected months rather than the full archive, so at 51 TB scale
the reclaim was modest. **Worth stating plainly: there is nothing comparable left to retire.** From
here, headroom comes from deleting resampled audio as each month completes.

### Resampled audio scales exactly with recording effort

**0.215 GB per hour recorded** — constant to three digits across all eight processed months, as
expected for 32 kHz 16-bit mono. So a month's footprint is predictable before running anything:

| Month | Size | Hours |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2015-07 | 17 GB | 78 |
| 2015-08 | 136 GB | 629 |
| 2015-09 | 155 GB | 719 |
| 2015-10 | 150 GB | ~697 |
| 2018-04 | 155 GB | 720 |
| 2018-05 | 160 GB | 744 |
| 2020-10 | 160 GB | 743 |
| 2024-09 | 97 GB | 450 |
| 2026-04 | 151 GB | 702 |

**A full 31-day month is ~160 GB. All 130 months at once would be ~20 TB — which does not fit.**
Hence the working rule: resample a month, analyse it, then delete the audio once findings, figures
and the coverage record are committed. The audio is always regenerable from `PAM_Archive`.

**The permanent artifacts are trivial by comparison: ~1.6 GB of embeddings per month, ~210 GB for
all 130 months.** Storage will never constrain the outputs — only the transient audio.

### Per-month time cost (measured)

| Stage | Cost |
|---|---|
| Resample (SoX, 8 parallel jobs) | **~4.5 h wall-clock**, but only **2.6% CPU** — it is network-bound on the share, not compute-bound |
| Embed (Perch V2, GB10 GPU) | **~30–40 min**, ~225 windows/s |
| Inference, both models | **~7 s** |
| **Review by ear** | **~25–30 min** for a month with an encounter |

**Review is the only stage that cannot be automated or parallelised.** Across ~130 months it is
roughly **50 hours of listening** — the real budget line, and the reason the two-pass zoom-in
protocol (§4) matters: it spends that time where the calls actually are.

## 9. Two recorder artifacts worth your confirmation

**Weekly clock resync.** April 2018 shows a 3 s filename-timestamp overlap at `07:59:14` on
**Apr 1, 8, 15, 22, 29**; May 2018 shows 2 s on **May 6, 13, 20, 27**. A weekly event at the
same second of day looks like a scheduled clock correction — the oscillator drifts, gets
reset, and the stamps compress while the audio stays continuous. **Is that right?** At 2–3 s
it cannot affect a 5 s analysis window, but we'd like to describe it correctly.

**Restart-and-resume.** September shows three 6–8 s overlaps each immediately followed by a
19–21 s gap (9/20, 9/24, 9/28) — a different signature from the 2018 weekly pattern.

## 10. Possible corroboration from sighting records — WE NEED YOUR HELP TO VERIFY

**Nothing in this section is established. It is a lead, and we cannot check it ourselves.**

Our three acoustic detection days are **2015-09-16, 2015-09-17, and 2015-09-28** (times in §2).
Informal searching turns up claims that Monterey Bay saw notable Bigg's killer whale activity in
exactly that window — and specifically that **CA95A1 / the CA95A matriline** was tracked in the bay
around September 16–17, with a separate encounter reported on September 28.

**We have not been able to source any of this to a citable record.** The material we found came
back with citations that did not match their claims (one "sighting" reference pointed at an
unrelated news story), which is the signature of generated rather than retrieved text. **We are
therefore treating the pod identifications as unverified and are not putting them in the project
record.** They are repeated here only so you can confirm or dismiss them.

### What we would like to ask you

1. **Do CKWP records show Bigg's killer whale sightings in Monterey Bay on 2015-09-16, 09-17, or
   09-28?** If so, which matrilines? Is CA95A / CA95A1 among them?
2. **Can you connect us with whoever holds the actual sighting logs?** Nancy Black / Monterey Bay
   Whale Watch / the California Killer Whale Project are the authoritative record for Monterey Bay
   Bigg's, but the logs are not easy to find from outside. **A warm introduction would help more
   than anything else here.**
3. **Could we get the sighting record as a DATASET rather than per-date lookups?** This is the point
   that matters most for the campaign. Correlating acoustics with sightings is already the top item
   on the roadmap; across ~130 months, a joinable table of dated sightings is the **join key for the
   entire seasonal analysis**. Confirming three days is useful; having the whole log is
   transformative.

### Why it would matter if it checks out

- **Three acoustic encounters matching three documented sighting days would be strong external
  validation** of the whole pipeline — resample → embed → classify → review. So far every
  confirmation has come from Duane's ear alone.
- **The September 28 co-occurrence would stop being a coincidence.** We confirmed **13 dolphin calls
  interleaved with 9 orca calls across 05:05–10:56** that morning (§2). Reports we cannot verify
  describe Bigg's *hunting dolphins* that day. If the sighting record supports it, that acoustic
  pattern becomes a **documented predation event**, which is a considerably stronger result than
  "orcas and dolphins were both present."
- **Same for September 16–17**, where common dolphins are mentioned and we have dolphin calls on
  both days — including one **10 seconds after an orca call in the same recording**.

### One caveat we would want in any write-up

2015 was the peak of the North Pacific marine heatwave ("the warm blob"). If Monterey Bay saw
anomalous warm water and unusual marine mammal distribution that autumn, then **September 2015 may
not be a representative month**, and an interannual comparison across the 11-year archive will need
to say so. You would know better than we would how much weight that deserves.

**One claim we would flag as needing particular scrutiny:** we also saw an assertion that the
endangered, fish-eating **Southern Resident L-Pod** was moving along the coast near the bay in the
same window. Southern Residents in Monterey Bay would be notable in itself, and it matters here
because a fish-eating ecotype vocalises very differently from Bigg's — and `orca_v10` is trained on
Bigg's. If there is anything to it, it changes how we read these detections. We suspect there is
not, but we cannot rule it out.
